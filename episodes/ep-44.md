With Rahul out traveling, Shimin and Dan open with Meta's Muse Spark Open, an 816B-parameter open-weight model that runs on two Mac Studios, and a former OpenAI engineer charged with hiding a recorder, wrapped in toilet paper, in an executive conference room. Post Processing starts with Andrej Karpathy's napkin math on who actually uses frontier AI, then reads Anthropic's "How employment is changing at Anthropic," a rare look at how a frontier lab works now. ## Takeaways

- **Meta is still shipping open weights, but the Chinese models still lead.** Muse Spark Open has 816B total parameters, 42B active, and a 1M-token context window. Meta calls it "the most capable US open-weight model to date" and says it's the first model in its class to run on a desktop: two M5 Ultra Mac Studios, which together hold the whole model in a terabyte of unified memory. It ships under the Muse Model License with limited commercial terms, so it's open-weight, not open source. It scores 39 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, level with Mistral Large 4 Preview and ahead of NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra and Thinking Machines' Inkling. But Qwen3.8, Kimi K3, and GLM-5.3 all score higher, and on Meta's own two coding benchmarks Kimi K3 and GLM 5.3 both beat it. Shimin gives Meta credit for sticking with open weights after Llama 4's troubles and the leadership change that brought in Alexandr Wang. In his view, Meta is one of the few labs that has both a closed frontier model (Muse Wonder) and a respectable open one. Neither host can work out who the two-Mac-Studio setup is for. *(On air we put Nemotron and Inkling in "the 30s and twenties"; both are in the 20s. "Beats Qwen 3.8 and GLM 5.3" holds only on some of Meta's selected benchmarks.)*
- **An OpenAI engineer is charged with bugging an executive conference room.** Prosecutors say Abdulrahman Alfozan, a 35-year-old Saudi national who had worked at OpenAI since July 2025, hid a recorder behind a TV in an executive conference room he wasn't authorized to use. It drew power from the TV's USB port and was wrapped in toilet paper. Security found it on September 30. On October 5 he left a bag in the office holding his wallet, his ID, and a second recording device. Investigators found prompts on a ChatGPT account tied to his wife asking how to build a continuous audio recorder and encrypt the SD card. The FBI complaint names the hardware as a Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense, about $14. Dan, who owns a few Seeed boards himself, wonders why OpenAI felt the need to say it used its models to identify the device. Alfozan is charged with intercepting oral communications, and a judge denied his release on October 19. Prosecutors say a member of the Saudi royal family repeatedly tried to recruit him to an AI venture, and that a Saudi military official bought new one-way tickets for his wife and young son after his arrest. His lawyer says the family's original tickets were for a two-month paternity leave. Dan suspects industrial espionage for a startup rather than a state operation. Prosecutors haven't said what the recordings were for. Shimin is waiting for the movie. *(On air we said the Saudi military bought the tickets. Alfozan booked the originals; the official bought new ones after the arrest. He was arrested and charged on October 9, and October 13 was his first court appearance.)*
- **To see where work is going, look at the 5,000.** Andrej Karpathy's napkin math has about 6 billion people who've barely touched an LLM, 1–2 billion using free chatbots like a better Google, around 20 million (0.2%) using frontier models professionally for math and code, and about 5,000 with access to frontier-grade systems internally. Shimin's point is that the future is unevenly distributed, so watch how frontier-lab employees work. Anthropic's "How employment is changing at Anthropic" is that view. By August, interactive agent hours outnumbered employee hours six to one, and 19 to one in research organizations. Commits per engineer are up 5.1x since December 2025. About a third of ML managers and a fifth of software engineering managers say they're hiring less because of AI. The product design team that wrote Claude.ai's greetings ("Hello night owl") now writes model evals and prompts: "Writers who used language to drive human behavior are now using it to drive Claude behavior." Hiring emphasizes "creative use of AI tools," and interview feedback rewards autonomy and judgment. Interactive sessions sit about 80% idle, waiting on a person, and one engineer says people are "bottlenecking the agents." Dan ties that to the code review bottleneck from [Episode 43](/episodes/43-openai-fires-its-safety-researchers-agentic-primitives-more-code-not-more-software/): deciding what to build is still a human process, and it takes time. Shimin doesn't think 5x the commits makes everyone a 10x engineer. The report's conclusion matches what the show keeps seeing: individual contributors orchestrating teams of agents start to look like managers. The difference at Anthropic, Dan suspects, is the token budget. *(On air we said commits rose 5.1 percent. It's 5.1x.)*
- **Parsimony measures how much code AI coding agents add.** Existing benchmarks measure solve rate and cost but don't punish excess code. Parsimony, an independent project, re-scores published benchmark patches by counting syntax units (calls, names, literals, statements) from the parsed AST, with docstrings stripped. It compares each model's net additions with what peer models added on the same solved tasks. On the default DeepSWE board, where every model runs on the same mini-SWE-agent harness, Claude Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 5 come in 12–14% under the peer average, Claude Opus 5 about 9% over, and the closed Muse Spark 1.1 and 1.2 write 29–34% more than peers. The author's early GPT-6 runs through Codex look leaner still, but they cover only 5–10 tasks and aren't ranked. The page's own caveats are that units measure size, not readability or design, and that models that write more code also tend to solve more tasks. Dan still wants to track it, because bloated code is harder to maintain even when an LLM is the one maintaining it. Shimin wants to compare two models' patches for the same task, and Dan suggests diffing their ASTs by node type. *(On air we called the GPT-6 models the winners and said Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 5 were negative. The GPT-6 results are unranked, and the two Claude models sit at 0.86–0.88x.)*
- **Neural networks may be cleaner than interpretability assumed.** Nick Cammarata co-wrote Distill's 2020 "Zoom In," which made polysemantic neurons famous: InceptionV1's neuron 4e:55 responds to cat faces, fronts of cars, and cat legs. Anthropic's 2022 "Toy Models of Superposition" explained such neurons as models packing in more features than they have dimensions, and the field's fix became sparse autoencoders. Golden Gate Claude was the showcase: a feature from a roughly 34-million-feature SAE on Claude 3 Sonnet, clamped to 10x its maximum activation, and never a single neuron. Now at Goodfire, Cammarata argues in "Neural Networks Are Already Clean" that most polysemanticity had ordinary causes: bad tools, bad explanations, and bad learning from architecture and data. Superposition should be the last hypothesis, not the first. With modern architectural fixes, 99–100% of neurons in InceptionV1's last layer become easy to interpret. A frontier-model neuron that fires on "pr," on Base64 strings, and on Bengali turns out to mean "pr" in all three. One neuron that detected "iy" grew over training to detect "diy" and then "do it yourself," which he calls recursive generalization. In a preliminary audit of Qwen3-32B, about 90% of neuron poles have one main job. He calls the essay a working hypothesis, not a claim that every neuron is clean. Dan says it finally made the replacement model behind [Episode 29](/episodes/29-fable-5-mythos-5-launch-meta-ai-hack-llms-as-black-boxes-future-of-agents/)'s circuit-tracing discussion click. Then Shimin steers Gemma 2 9B live on Neuronpedia: a "cat mode" preset turns a request for a podcast mascot into a playful, aloof cat, while the unsteered model suggests a coding spider. If neurons are readable, Shimin argues, you could trace where a model decided to misbehave anyway, as in the Hugging Face hack from [Episode 35](/episodes/35-kimi-k3-qwen-3-8-openai-agent-hacks-hugging-face-harness-handbook-claude-s-values/), or tune down sycophancy. Dan asks why a model trained on nearly all human writing produces cable-news headlines instead of Hemingway. Shimin's answer: next-token loss rewards the probable, and good art is by definition an outlier. *(On air we said the Golden Gate feature was multiplied 100 times, that the "pr" neuron fired on Sanskrit, and that iy, diy, and "do it yourself" lived in successive layers. The sourced versions are above.)*
- **Nobody can tell you when to restart a session.** Context tooling has improved since Dan's first rant about it. Harnesses like Pi can drop tool calls from a session, compaction is less bad, and spec-driven workflows make restarting easy. But tokens are being counted carefully again and the frontier models cost more, so the question is now cost. A session can be nowhere near full and still "have enough context to be dangerous," and nothing estimates whether rebuilding it from your tickets would cost less than carrying it. Dan wants predictive tooling for that, and might write it himself. He also admits on the record that he's been giving work to Sonnet, and that 5.5 does better than he expected. Shimin's pattern is a stronger model as the orchestrator with Sonnet as the worker.
- **CoreWeave's bonds rattle the neoclouds, and the clock moves to 4:00.** On October 13, Chamath Palihapitiya posted a chart showing the yield on $1.25B of CoreWeave bonds due 2032 touching 15% and closing at a high of 14.5%, and asked whether the AI build-out is creating a private credit bubble. Elon Musk replied "Yikes." CoreWeave's stock fell 15% the next day. Nebius fell 11%, Oracle 9%, and DigitalOcean and TeraWulf more than 10%. CEO Michael Intrator said the market "misunderstands and acts irrationally." CoreWeave's Q2 interest expense was $640M, up from $267M a year earlier, and it lost $626M in the quarter while revenue more than doubled to $2.58B. Its debt went from $14.6B to $51.6B in a year. J.P. Morgan puts large US investment banks' and MUFG's potential exposure at about $1–3B and calls it "well managed and very well secured," while expecting CoreWeave's free cash flow to stay negative until 2030. CoreWeave's 2031 notes touched almost 16%. Shimin is less reassured. He quotes James Carville wanting to come back as the bond market, says the bond market souring on neoclouds matters more than the stock price, and notes that banks aren't supposed to hold this paper for long. Dan connects it to Post Processing: if all this code isn't making anyone a 10x developer, the debt has to be repaid out of gains that may not come. The clock moves 15 seconds closer, to 4:00. *(On air we put the $626M loss in Q1 and had Oracle down more than 10%.)*
