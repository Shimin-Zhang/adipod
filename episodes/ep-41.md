Shimin, Dan, and Rahul are back after two weeks off, and there's a lot to catch up on. The News covers Meta's Muse, which picked up 3.4M downloads in three weeks and shipped with a setting a prompt injection can use to swap its own endpoint and leak your auth token. DHH tells Rails World that writing code by hand is no longer economically productive and that 37signals is moving to Rust. CNN reports a military near-miss: a chatbot misidentified a ship as Chinese and possibly carrying nuclear material, and the boarding was called off at the last minute. A lightning round runs through MongoDB's CEO leaving for Meta, OpenAI solving a Millennium Prize problem, Anthropic's $2T IPO filing, Sonnet and Opus 5.5, and the JEV "system zero" classifier. Model Review takes on GPT-6 Astra, which brings strong computer use, the debut of Shimin's Shakespeare benchmark, and a looped transformer that reasons without chain of thought. Post Processing covers Epoch AI's plunging price of thought, Sean Goedecke on telling agents the why, and Baldur Bjarnason's LLMentalist effect, picking up the persuasion thread from [Episode 32](/episodes/32-glm-5-2-undercuts-opus-self-rewriting-harness-ai-out-persuades-humans-prompt-injection-as-role-confusion/). Two Minutes to Midnight covers NVIDIA as the central bank of AI, Oracle's force majeure on Project Jupiter, and AMD's $8.2B purchase of revenue-free World Labs. The clock holds at 4:15.

## Takeaways

- **Muse's worst bug is a setting, not the model.** Meta's model is good: Muse scored 48 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, between Opus 5 Max and GPT-6 Sol Max. But Muse can change its own settings, and one of those settings is its endpoint. A prompt injection can point it at an attacker's server, which then gets your auth token. On the Mac app that means every account you connected and every folder you granted access to. Add reports that it uploads iMessages anyway and that "AI" reservations go through a human call center. Shimin's summary: three OpenClaws stacked in a trench coat, with Meta's distribution behind it.
- **DHH said the quiet part at Rails World.** The creator of Rails told a Rails conference that only about 3% of the code he wrote in the last two years was Ruby. 37signals is moving back ends to Rust and front ends to native apps wherever it can, and hand-written code "is no longer an economically productive enterprise." Shimin doesn't disagree with him on this one. The open question is who Rails is for once its own creator's company stops using it.
- **Developers were the canary, and the gas is reaching everyone else.** In the CNN story, a chatbot's misidentification became a distributed intelligence report and nearly a boarding, inside an active war. Rahul's fix is to cite your sources, read them, and use source-grounded tools for anything critical. Shimin's point is that every industry now faces the hallucination and human-in-the-loop questions developers have been working through for two years, and each will answer them differently.
- **GPT-6 Astra is great to watch and harder to monitor.** It's the best computer-use and visual model the hosts have seen. Shimin's new Shakespeare benchmark (an SVG animation of Hamlet's soliloquy with Web Audio) shows a big jump over the previous GPT generation. The worry is the looped transformer, which can run a sequence through the same blocks several times before emitting a token. That puts reasoning outside chain of thought, where monitors can't read it. It's a close cousin of the layer-duplication trick from [Episode 26](/episodes/26-llm-neural-anatomy-with-david-noel-ng-forward-deployed-everybody-running-llms-at-home/). OpenAI says it capped the loops for monitorability. Neel Nanda worries about a race to the bottom between labs that won't.
- **Build as if tokens were 1000x cheaper.** Epoch AI finds the cost of fixed performance falling about 47% a quarter, roughly 13x a year. That's faster than DNA sequencing, Moore's law, lithium batteries, or electricity. The cost of an o3-level GPQA Diamond answer fell by orders of magnitude in under 18 months, and new models shed about two-thirds of their launch premium within months. Math costs fall fastest, unstructured puzzles slowest. Shimin's corollary: anything with a verifiable answer that can be RL'd may stop being scarce within a few years, and value moves to unverifiable work like taste, judgment, and creativity. Rahul's caveat is that in the real world intelligence is rarely the bottleneck.
- **Tell agents the why, then refactor your old skills.** Sean Goedecke argues current models need your priorities and long-term goals more than step-by-step instructions. Developers are doing the team-building side of management now: shared vocabulary, priorities, coding style. Because models keep improving, the over-specified instructions you wrote into skills and CLAUDE.md files six months ago can start to hurt, so prune them. Shimin's argument for keeping skills under human control is that skills, like software, are calcified decisions, and an agent that edits them can quietly overwrite trade-offs you made on purpose. Dan's "Friday summary" skill is the example: a template plus "always add, don't subtract," turning daily notes into a weekly status report for his boss.
- **The LLMentalist effect works even if the model reasons.** Baldur Bjarnason's 2023 essay maps chatbots onto the psychic's con. The audience selects itself (skeptics don't use chatbots), hype does the priming, and your prompt picks you out as the mark. Barnum statements, a "would you like me to…?" validation loop, and RLHF finish the job, since RLHF is humans picking the answers that feel most right to them. Dan doesn't buy the essay's "no intelligence" premise, but its persuasion mechanics still apply. Shimin played an AI cold-read game on vacation, and it guessed Disco Elysium. He remembers that hit and none of the misses. You have to hold two ideas at once: these are matrix multiplications, and they are trained to act human, so expect human-like effects.
- **NVIDIA lends, Oracle stalls, AMD buys a name, and the clock holds at 4:15.** The Economist calls NVIDIA the central bank of AI. It made about 90 startup investments last year and has agreed to about 60 more this year, and it narrows the gap between neoclouds and hyperscalers on borrowing costs (CoreWeave borrowed at almost double Alphabet's 5.7%). Dan adds the part the article skips: networking, where NVIDIA still has almost no competition. Oracle invoked force majeure on Project Jupiter over power delays, and AMD paid $8.2B, about 36x what World Labs raised, for a company with no reported revenue. Oracle is the only news pushing the clock forward, so it stays at 4:15 until the IPO numbers land.
